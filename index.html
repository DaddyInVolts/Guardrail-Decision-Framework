Section 1

Problem & Opportunity

Problem Statement

Feature teams running experiments on ExP get rich scorecards but no consistent answer to the question that actually blocks ship: "Will this regress a guardrail metric?" Today, each team reads p-values and confidence intervals against their own ad-hoc rules of thumb. A non-significant +0.4% crash-rate movement is shipped by one team and blocked by another. Underpowered guardrails are treated as "passing" when in reality they're uninformative. The result is preventable user-facing regressions, inconsistent ship bars across the division, and data-scientist toil reviewing every borderline scorecard.

Opportunity

IDEAS Exp has the opportunity to evaluate ~3,000+ experiments per month. An ExP study of 240 randomly sampled "shipped" experiments from Q1 2026 found that 11% of experiments shipped on signals that were statistically uninformative on harm. A deterministic, auditable Guardrail Decision Framework — built from rule-based logic — turns guardrails from a "vibes check" into a contract: each guardrail metric returns a decision with an action and a reason, and the scorecards roll up to a single guardrail verdict.

Section 2

Goals & Success Metrics

Goals

  1. Drive ≥95% of scorecards with at least one guardrail metric toward a "decided" state of safe or not-safe — leaving only genuinely-underpowered cases as wait.
  2. Reduce harmful ship decisions based on underpowered data.
  3. Have the same guardrails and decision criteria for the entire division.
  4. Replace naïve guardrail interpretation with a deterministic ruling per guardrail and a single roll-up verdict for the rollout / experiment.

Success Metrics

Primary Metric
Guardrail Verdict Coverage
≥95% of scorecard evaluations return a safe or not-safe decision when at least one guardrail is configured.

Explicit Non-Goals

Product ship-decision logic — covered by the partner product teams; this spec is division guardrails-only
SRM detection or sample-ratio mismatch handling — already a blocking pre-check upstream of this framework
Segment or slice-based guardrails — aggregate rules all; slices cannot outweigh an aggregate decision
GPT scorecard narrative — this spec is the rule-based source of truth; GPT summarization consumes this decision, not the other way around
Automatic threshold authoring — practical-significance thresholds (the smallest meaningful change) are defined per guardrail; this framework consumes those definitions, it does not generate them
Any UX for this feature — all decisions will be presented in the detail page
Section 3

User Scenarios

Scenario 1 — "The Sprint-Heavy PM" (Primary)

Jackson is a feature PM with three experiments analyzing in parallel. He opens Expedite for his checkout-flow redesign. Expedite has two guardrails: % Users Acquired Paid for Consumers and % Users With License Request Pending for Commercial audiences. Today he'd read the colors on the xCard and guess if they were not stat-sig. With the Guardrail Decision Framework he sees a single panel: Guardrails: Safe to ship, with two cards beneath — % Users Acquired Paid (powered, not stat-sig, CI rules out the configured 0.03% meaningful harm threshold) and % Users With License Request Pending (powered, +0.2% stat-sig but well under the meaningful 5% tolerance). One sentence each. He ships in under 90 seconds.

Scenario 2 — "The Borderline Investigator"

Marcus runs a search-ranking experiment. The framework returns Do not ship — 7-day retention. The card shows: observed −0.4% retention rate, p=0.18, CI = [−0.15%, +0.95%], threshold = −0.1%. The CI does not rule out a −0.15% spike (above the −0.1% tolerance), so even though it's not stat-sig, the framework flags it. Marcus pivots: he doesn't ship, doesn't argue with the verdict, and asks DS whether to extend the experiment or rework the change. The verdict gave him a sharp question, not a fuzzy one.

Scenario 3 — "The Noob Squad"

A new Copilot Growth squad runs their first ExP scorecard on a new Iris campaign. They have no product scorecard. The framework returns a dozen guardrails for their experiment — balanced between Consumer and Commercial, performance and growth — with sensible thresholds. No false green, no silent ship because they didn't make a configuration.

Scenario 4 — "The Documented Override"

An eng manager hits a not-safe ruling via email on a guardrail she knows is broken: a logging change in the treatment cell has temporarily inflated the "UPLT" metric definition. In Expedite she changes "Stop" to "Ship"; a modal requires a written justification (min 10 chars), a mitigation, and a follow-up. The override is logged with her identity, timestamp, and the original verdict; her data-science partner and the experiment-owner alias are notified automatically. The red banner remains on the detail page for audit, but ship is unblocked.

Edge Case — Underpowered guardrail, no harm signal

A small experiment (5% traffic, 4 days) has an underpowered DSAT guardrail: threshold = 0.2%. Observed +0.1%, p=0.40, CI = [−0.4%, +0.6%]. The framework returns Underpowered — extend test or ship with monitoring rather than safe or not-safe, because the CI does not rule out the configured 0.2% threshold. The reasoning is one line; the next-action recommendation is "Extend ≥7 days to reach MDE ≤ 0.2%."

Section 4

Requirements

Priority ID Requirement Acceptance Criteria Notes
P0 R-01 Expedite Admins identify guardrail metrics and their meaningful thresholds. Each guardrail must declare (a) a direction — higher-is-better or lower-is-better — and (b) a practical-significance threshold Δ (a non-negative number expressing the smallest harmful effect size the team cares about). The framework consumes these; it does not author them. A variety of guardrails representing Performance and Growth variables across Consumer and Commercial dimensions are labeled in an editable .md file. Guardrails missing direction or Δ surface a setup error and are excluded from the verdict roll-up. Stakeholders should sign off on the guardrails chosen.
P0 R-02 Each metric identified as a Guardrail is evaluated independently and returns a verdict of safe / extend / not-safe. For a scorecard with N guardrails, exactly N verdicts are emitted, one per guardrail, each with a single-line reason string. Depends on guardrail identification.
P0 R-03 For each guardrail, compute the CI safety check based on direction: lower-is-better → CI_upper < +Δ; higher-is-better → CI_lower > −Δ. The check answers "does the CI rule out a meaningful degradation?" CI check returns boolean pass/fail; expression matches the published formula 1:1 in QA tests across 50 example scorecards. Ex: Error Rate (lower is better). Result: +0.1%, p=0.55, CI = [−0.15%, +0.25%]. Threshold: 0.3%. Check: 0.25% < 0.3%? → Yes. Worst case is within tolerance. Safe to ship.
P0 R-04 Powered + stat-sig + harmful direction → not safe — "[guardrail metric] harmed". "Powered" = pre-experiment MDE at 80% power ≤ Δ. The verdict and message are deterministic; no GPT override. Unit tests cover crash-rate +0.6% p=0.01, MDE=0.2%, Δ=0.5% → not safe with the exact message string.
P0 R-05 Powered + not stat-sig + CI passes → Safe ("CI rules out degradation beyond Δ"). Powered + not stat-sig + CI fails → Not safe ("CI does not rule out meaningful degradation"). Unit tests cover both branches with scorecard examples. The "rules out harm" guarantee is the framework's core promise.
P0 R-06 Underpowered + not stat-sig + CI passes → Underpowered ("CI rules out severe harm — extend or ship with monitoring"). Underpowered + not stat-sig + CI fails → Not safe ("CI suggests possible harm"). Unit tests confirm that a CI passing Δ still yields Underpowered (not safe) when MDE > Δ — the underpowered case never returns Safe. This is one of the largest changes that takes us from 99% flat no-decision to >95% safe / not-safe.
P0 R-07 Roll-up rule: scorecard-level guardrail verdict = Stop if any guardrail is not safe; else Hold if any guardrail is extend; else Ship. The roll-up is shown in the Decision card. Mixed-verdict fixtures produce the correct roll-up; the worst single guardrail (measured as largest delta between CI and Δ) is always cited in the roll-up reason.
P1 R-09 Pre-experiment power is computed per guardrail as MDE at 80% power using control-arm variance. "Powered" = MDE ≤ Δ (absolute value); "Underpowered" = MDE > Δ. The MDE value is displayed on every guardrail card. Power calc matches the source-doc formulas to ≤1% relative error on 100 scorecards. Post-hoc power is explicitly not used.
P1 R-10 Powered + stat-sig + non-harmful direction with observed effect within Δ → Safe ("Stat-sig but within tolerance — safe to ship"). Treated as safe; the guardrail is not asked to move in the helpful direction. Ex: +0.1% error rate, p=0.01, Δ=0.3% → Safe with the precise reason string. Prevents false-Reds on tiny but stat-sig movements, such as in event-triggered scorecards.
P1 R-11 Borderline stat-sig zone (0.05 < p ≤ 0.1) on a powered guardrail in the harmful direction → Extend ("Guardrail at risk — get IDEAS Exp office hours review") regardless of CI check. Verdict is Extend (not Safe) in this zone even when the CI nominally rules out Δ; reason cites the borderline p-value.
P1 R-14 Multi-variant experiments evaluate guardrails per T/C cell independently. One verdict per variant is shown when using the variant filter at the top of the page; there is no roll-up across all variants. 3-arm example (variant A — safe, B — not safe, C — extend); variant pages display individually depending on filter selection.

Section 6

Open Questions & Risks

Open Questions

Q1: What's the canonical list of "platform default" guardrails?

We need to publish the canonical default list of guardrails — with default Δ values that work for the majority of feature areas — and get stakeholder sign-off.

Q2: Should the guardrail table be auditable?
Q3: When a change to the guardrails (in count or threshold) is made, is it applied retroactively, or only proactively?
Q4: What should the behavior be when BH-adjusted p-value and CI rules disagree?

Use an arbitrator criterion (e.g., pMove), or pick one rule to be more important?

Risks

Risk 1: False Reds Drive Override Fatigue

Likelihood: Medium · Impact: High. If the framework returns not safe too aggressively — particularly on widely-deployed metrics with noisy small movements — teams will reflexively override and the audit log becomes meaningless. Mitigation: (1) R-10 "stat-sig within tolerance → Safe" prevents the common false-Red on tiny movements; (2) track the False-Red Rate guardrail metric (target ≤5%); (3) review overrides weekly for the first two quarters and tune thresholds where patterns emerge.

Risk 2: Underpowered Guardrails Silently Permit Harm

Likelihood: High · Impact: High. If a small experiment has wide CIs on a guardrail, today's ad-hoc readings often call it "fine." The framework's R-06 explicitly returns Underpowered (not Safe) for the underpowered + non-stat-sig + CI-passes case, but Underpowered is easy to dismiss. Mitigation: (1) Roll-up Underpowered blocks one-click ship in the UI (forces explicit acknowledgment); (2) recommend a concrete extend-the-test next action with the MDE math shown; (3) instrument adoption of the "extend" action vs Underpowered ships.
