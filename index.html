Section 1

Problem & Opportunity

Problem Statement

Feature teams running experiments on ExP get rich scorecards but no consistent answer to the question that actually blocks ship: "Will this regress a guardrail metric?" Today, each team reads p-values and confidence intervals against their own ad-hoc rules of thumb. A non-significant +0.4% crash-rate movement is shipped by one team and blocked by another. Underpowered guardrails are treated as "passing" when in reality they're uninformative. The result is preventable user-facing regressions, inconsistent ship bars across the org, and data-scientist toil reviewing every borderline scorecard.

Opportunity

ExP processes ~3,200 scorecards per week. Internal audit of 240 randomly sampled "shipped" experiments from Q1 2026 found that 11% had at least one guardrail with a confidence interval that did not rule out the team's stated practical-significance threshold — i.e., they shipped on signals that were statistically silent on harm. A deterministic, auditable Guardrail Decision Framework — built from the rule-based logic in Scorecard Decision Framework by ExP (Agrawal, 2026) — turns guardrails from a "vibes check" into a contract: each guardrail metric returns 🟢/🟡/🔴 with a one-line reason, and the scorecard rolls up to a single guardrail verdict. Expected impact: ↓ harmful ships, ↓ DS review queue (target: 30% fewer escalations on guardrail-only questions), ↑ consistency across teams.

Evidence

  • Audit: Q1 2026 ship-decision audit — 11% of 240 sampled shipped experiments had at least one guardrail CI that didn't rule out the team's own harm threshold
  • DS Workload: 38% of inbound DS scorecard reviews in Q1 were specifically "is this guardrail OK to ship?" — a deterministic ruling absorbs most of these
  • Prior Art: Kohavi et al., Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments (Ch. 8, Guardrail Metrics); Colin McFarland, Traffic Light Concept for Scorecard Summarization (2026)
  • Internal: Agrawal, Scorecard Decision Framework by ExP (4/27/2026) — defines the rule-based decision layer this spec scopes down to guardrails
Section 2

Goals & Success Metrics

Goals

  1. Replace ad-hoc guardrail interpretation with a deterministic 🟢/🟡/🔴 ruling per guardrail and a single roll-up verdict for the scorecard
  2. Cut data-scientist escalations for "is this guardrail OK to ship?" by ≥30% within one quarter of GA
  3. Drive ≥95% of scorecards with at least one guardrail toward a "decided" state (green or red) — leaving only genuinely-underpowered cases as yellow

Success Metrics

Primary Metric
Guardrail Verdict Coverage
Target: ≥95% of scorecards return a 🟢 or 🔴 (not 🟡) when ≥1 guardrail is configured · Baseline: ~62% deterministic today
Secondary Metric
DS Review Deflection
Target: −30% DS reviews tagged "guardrail check" within 90 days post-GA · Baseline: ~410 reviews/quarter
Guardrail Metric
False-Red Rate
Must not exceed: 5% of 🔴 rulings overturned on DS review · Tracked weekly via override audit log

Explicit Non-Goals

Primary-metric ship logic — covered by the broader ExP Scorecard Decision Framework; this spec is guardrails-only
SRM detection or sample-ratio mismatch handling — already a blocking pre-check upstream of this framework
Segment-of-interest (SOI) discovery / heterogeneous treatment effect modeling — separate workstream
LLM-generated scorecard narrative — this spec is the rule-based source of truth; LLM summarization consumes our verdict, not the other way around
Automatic threshold authoring — practical-significance thresholds are user-supplied per guardrail; this framework consumes them, not generates them
Section 3

User Scenarios

Scenario 1 — "The Sprint-Heavy PM" (Primary)

Priya is a feature PM with three experiments analyzing in parallel. She opens the scorecard for her checkout-flow redesign. Her primary metric is +2.3% revenue (stat-sig), but she has two guardrails: crash rate and p95 latency. Today she'd read the CIs herself and guess. With the Guardrail Decision Framework she sees a single panel: Guardrails: 🟢 Safe to ship, with two cards beneath — crash rate (powered, not stat-sig, CI rules out her configured 0.3% harm threshold) and p95 latency (powered, +0.2% stat-sig but well under her 5% tolerance). One sentence each. She ships in under 90 seconds.

Scenario 2 — "The Borderline Investigator"

Marcus runs a search-ranking experiment. The framework returns 🔴 Do not ship — error rate. The card shows: observed +0.4% error rate, p=0.18, CI = [−0.15%, +0.95%], threshold = 0.5%. The CI does not rule out a 0.95% spike (above his 0.5% tolerance), so even though it's not stat-sig, the framework flags it. Marcus pivots: he doesn't ship, doesn't argue with the verdict, and asks DS whether to extend the experiment or rework the change. The verdict gave him a sharp question, not a fuzzy one.

Scenario 3 — "The Empty-Slate Team"

A new feature team runs their first ExP scorecard. They haven't configured guardrails. The framework returns a 🟡 No guardrails configured roll-up with a recommendation panel: "Add crash rate, error rate, or p95 latency before promoting this experiment." A one-click "Add platform-default guardrails" action wires up the three defaults to their experiment with sensible thresholds, and the scorecard re-evaluates. No false green, no silent ship.

Scenario 4 — "The Documented Override"

An eng manager hits a 🔴 ruling on a guardrail she knows is broken: a logging change in the treatment cell has temporarily inflated the "error rate" metric definition. She clicks "Override → Ship anyway." A modal requires a written justification (min 80 chars) and a category (e.g., Metric measurement issue, Legal/compliance requirement). The override is logged with her identity, timestamp, and the original verdict; her data-science partner and the experiment-owner alias are notified automatically. The red banner remains on the scorecard for audit, but ship is unblocked.

Edge Case — Underpowered guardrail, no harm signal

A small-cell experiment (5% traffic, 4 days) has an underpowered crash-rate guardrail: MDE = 1.2%, threshold = 0.5%. Observed +0.1%, p=0.40, CI = [−0.4%, +0.6%]. The framework returns 🟡 Underpowered — extend test or ship with monitoring rather than green or red, because the CI does rule out severe harm (0.6% < 1.0% × 2) but does not rule out the configured 0.5% threshold. The reasoning is one line; the next-action recommendation is "Extend ≥7 days to reach MDE ≤ 0.5%."

Section 4

Requirements

Priority ID Requirement Acceptance Criteria Notes
P0 R-01 Each metric in the scorecard tagged Guardrail is evaluated independently and returns a verdict of 🟢 Green / 🟡 Yellow / 🔴 Red. The framework consumes ExP metric categories — guardrails are distinguished from primary, secondary, and debug metrics. For a scorecard with N guardrails, exactly N verdicts are emitted, one per guardrail, each with a single-line reason string Depends on ExP metric category metadata
P0 R-02 Each guardrail must declare (a) a direction — higher-is-better or lower-is-better — and (b) a practical-significance threshold Δ (a non-negative number expressing the smallest harmful effect size the team cares about). The framework consumes these; it does not author them. Guardrails missing direction or Δ surface a setup error and are excluded from the verdict roll-up Authoring UX is out of scope
P0 R-03 For each guardrail, compute the CI safety check based on direction: lower-is-better → CI_upper < +Δ; higher-is-better → CI_lower > −Δ. The check answers "does the CI rule out a meaningful degradation?" CI check returns boolean pass/fail; expression matches the published formula 1:1 in QA tests across 50 fixture scorecards Source: Agrawal 4/27, Type-A CI table
P0 R-04 Powered + stat-sig + harmful direction → 🔴 Red ("Do not ship — [metric] harmed"). "Powered" = pre-experiment MDE at 80% power ≤ Δ. The verdict and message are deterministic; no LLM override. Unit tests cover crash-rate +0.6% p=0.01, MDE=0.2%, Δ=0.5% → 🔴 with the exact message string
P0 R-05 Powered + not stat-sig + CI passes → 🟢 Green ("Safe — CI rules out degradation beyond Δ"). Powered + not stat-sig + CI fails → 🔴 Red ("CI does not rule out meaningful degradation"). Unit tests cover both branches with fixture scorecards drawn from the source-doc examples The "rules out harm" guarantee is the framework's core promise
P0 R-06 Underpowered + not stat-sig + CI passes → 🟡 Yellow ("Underpowered, but CI rules out severe harm — extend or ship with monitoring"). Underpowered + not stat-sig + CI fails → 🔴 Red ("CI suggests possible harm"). Unit tests confirm that a CI passing Δ still yields Yellow (not Green) when MDE > Δ — the under-powered case never returns Green Source: Agrawal 4/27, Type-B CI table
P0 R-07 Roll-up rule: scorecard-level guardrail verdict = 🔴 if any guardrail is 🔴; else 🟡 if any guardrail is 🟡; else 🟢. The roll-up is shown above the per-guardrail cards. Mixed-verdict fixtures produce the correct roll-up; the worst single guardrail is always cited in the roll-up reason
P0 R-08 If a scorecard has zero metrics tagged Guardrail, return roll-up = 🟡 with message "No guardrails configured — add crash rate, error rate, or latency before promoting." Offer a one-click action to add platform-default guardrails to the experiment. Empty-guardrail scorecards never return Green; default-guardrails action wires up the platform defaults and triggers re-evaluation Platform-default list maintained by ExP DS
P1 R-09 Pre-experiment power is computed per guardrail as MDE at 80% power using control-arm variance. "Powered" = MDE ≤ Δ; "Underpowered" = MDE > Δ. The MDE value is displayed on every guardrail card. Power calc matches the source-doc formulas to ≤1% relative error on 100 fixture scorecards Post-hoc power is explicitly not used
P1 R-10 Powered + stat-sig + non-harmful direction with observed effect within Δ → 🟢 Green Neutral ("Stat-sig but within tolerance — safe to ship"). Treated as safe; the guardrail is not asked to move in the helpful direction. Fixture: +0.1% error rate, p=0.01, Δ=0.3% → 🟢 Green Neutral with the precise reason string Prevents false-Reds on tiny but stat-sig movements
P1 R-11 Borderline stat-sig zone (0.05 < p ≤ 0.10) on a powered guardrail in the harmful direction → 🟡 Yellow ("Guardrail at risk — get DS review") regardless of CI check. Verdict is Yellow (not Green) in this zone even when the CI nominally rules out Δ; reason cites the borderline p-value Mirrors source-doc decision matrix row "Guardrail borderline"
P1 R-12 Override flow: from any 🔴 ruling the user can choose "Override and ship anyway." A modal requires (a) a category from a fixed list (Metric measurement issue, Legal/compliance requirement, Known false positive, Other) and (b) a written justification ≥ 80 characters. Override is rejected without both fields; with both, ship is unblocked while the 🔴 banner remains on the scorecard for audit Inspired by the "Ship despite Red" pattern from Expedite Experiment Detail spec
P1 R-13 Every override is logged with user identity, UTC timestamp, original verdict, justification, and category. Notifications fire to the experiment-owner alias and the experiment's DS contact within 60 seconds. Audit log entries match schema; notifications observed in DS partner inbox in QA Notifications respect user prefs
P2 R-14 Multi-variant experiments evaluate guardrails per treatment cell independently. One verdict per variant is shown; the scorecard roll-up is the worst across all variants. 3-arm fixture (variant A 🟢, B 🔴, C 🟡) → roll-up 🔴 citing variant B; variant cards display individually
P2 R-15 Segment-aware guardrails: if a guardrail is 🟢 in aggregate but 🔴 in any pre-registered critical segment, downgrade the per-guardrail verdict to 🟡 ("Aggregate safe but [segment] degraded — consider targeted rollout"). Aggregate Green + segment Red fixtures yield 🟡 (not 🟢) with the recommended-action text; segments not pre-registered are excluded Multiple-testing correction handled upstream (BH per metric)
P2 R-16 Each guardrail card exposes a "Why this verdict?" expandable section showing: observed effect, p-value, CI, threshold Δ, direction, MDE, and the exact decision rule that was applied (e.g., "Powered + non-stat-sig + CI passes → Green"). The exact rule string appears verbatim from a single shared dictionary across all calling surfaces (UI, API, audit log) Makes the framework auditable end-to-end
Section 5

Design Direction — if applicable

UX Flow

Scorecard Computed → Rule Engine Evaluates Each Guardrail → Roll-up Verdict + Per-Guardrail Cards Render in Scorecard "Guardrails" Tab
User Flow: Scorecard → "Guardrails" Tab → Read Roll-up Banner → Scan Per-Metric Cards → [Why?] for Reasoning → [Ship] or [Override] (on Red)

Prototype: (to be linked — TBD)

Page Layout

  • Roll-up Banner (top): Full-width 🟢/🟡/🔴 banner with a single sentence ("Safe to ship — 3 guardrails pass" / "Do not ship — error rate harmed" / "Underpowered — extend test"). Color matches verdict; on Red, a primary "Override and ship anyway" button is right-aligned (visible but secondary in weight).
  • Per-Guardrail Cards: One card per guardrail under the banner. Each card shows: metric name, verdict pill (color matches roll-up palette), observed effect with direction arrow, threshold Δ, MDE at 80% power, and a one-line reason. A "Why?" disclosure expands the full decision row (R-16).
  • Empty State (no guardrails configured): Yellow banner + a prominent recommendation card: "Add platform defaults — crash rate, error rate, p95 latency — with one click." The Ship button is disabled until either guardrails are added or an override is recorded.
  • Override Modal: Opens on "Override and ship anyway." Required: category dropdown (4 options) + justification textarea (≥80 chars). Confirm button disabled until both are valid. Modal footer notes the audit-log notification list.

Key Interaction Notes

  • Verdict pills are color-only — the icon (✓ / ! / ✕) and the word "Safe" / "At risk" / "Blocked" appear together for accessibility. Color is never the sole signal.
  • The roll-up banner reason cites the worst guardrail by name, e.g., "Do not ship — error rate CI does not rule out a 0.6% spike." Users can jump to that card with a single click.
  • Hovering the threshold Δ shows a tooltip with the rationale ("Smallest harmful effect this team cares about — set at experiment authoring time").
  • "Override and ship anyway" never removes the red banner from the scorecard. It appends an "Overridden" chip with the user's identity and timestamp; the original verdict remains visible for audit.

Edge Cases & Empty States

  • No guardrails configured: Yellow roll-up with the platform-defaults CTA (per R-08). Verdict is never 🟢 in this state.
  • Guardrail with missing direction or threshold: Card shows a setup-error state and is excluded from the roll-up (per R-02); user is linked to the metric-authoring surface to fix.
  • Borderline p-value (0.05–0.10): Yellow card with "Guardrail at risk — get DS review" reason and a built-in "Assign to DS" action (per R-11).
  • Multi-variant scorecard: Per-variant verdict pills appear on each guardrail card; roll-up cites the worst variant (per R-14).
  • Aggregate Green but critical segment Red: Per-guardrail card downgrades to Yellow with "Aggregate safe but [segment] degraded — consider targeted rollout" (per R-15).
  • Underpowered Yellow: Card shows MDE > Δ and recommends concrete next action ("Extend ≥7 days to reach MDE ≤ Δ").

Accessibility

  • Verdict pills meet AA contrast for the corresponding palette pair; the verdict word and icon are read aloud by screen readers ("Guardrail error rate, verdict Blocked, do not ship").
  • The roll-up banner is the first focusable region on the Guardrails tab; tabbing reaches each guardrail card in document order.
  • The override modal is fully keyboard-operable; Esc cancels, Enter confirms only when both fields are valid. Form errors are announced via aria-live.
Section 6

Open Questions & Risks

Open Questions

Q1: What's the canonical list of "platform default" guardrails for teams with none configured?

R-08 calls for a one-click "Add platform defaults" action. We need ExP DS to publish the canonical default list (likely crash rate, error rate, p95 latency) with default Δ values that work for the majority of feature areas, and a process for area-specific overrides. Owner: Ankita Agrawal (ExP DS). ETA for decision: June 2026.

Q2: Where do practical-significance thresholds Δ get authored, and by whom?

This spec consumes Δ but does not author it. We need to settle whether Δ is a per-metric platform default (set once by ExP DS), a per-experiment override (set by the PM at experiment-design time), or both. Strong leaning: both, with per-metric default + per-experiment optional override. Owner: Dylan Daly (PM) + Ankita Agrawal (DS).

Q3: Should overrides require a second approver (two-person rule) for high-traffic experiments?

R-12 / R-13 require justification + audit but not a co-signer. For experiments above a certain user count or revenue exposure, a two-person rule may be safer. Risk of adding it: friction, especially for clear-cut measurement-error overrides. Current leaning: Ship V1 with single-user override + audit; add two-person rule in V2 if overrides are abused.

Q4: How are multiple-testing corrections (BH per metric across segments) communicated to the user?

R-15 references BH-corrected segment guardrails handled "upstream." We need to confirm the segment-of-interest pipeline already applies BH per guardrail before this framework reads results, and decide whether the framework UI mentions BH to users or hides it. Owner: Harrison Wang (ExP DS).

Risks

Risk 1: False Reds Drive Override Fatigue

Likelihood: Medium · Impact: High. If the framework returns 🔴 too aggressively — particularly on widely-deployed metrics with noisy small movements — teams will reflexively override and the audit log becomes meaningless. Mitigation: (1) R-10 "stat-sig within tolerance → Green Neutral" prevents the common false-Red on tiny movements; (2) track the False-Red Rate guardrail metric (target ≤5%); (3) review overrides weekly with ExP DS for the first two quarters and tune thresholds where patterns emerge.

Risk 2: Underpowered Guardrails Silently Permit Harm

Likelihood: High · Impact: High. If a small-cell experiment has wide CIs on a guardrail, today's ad-hoc readings often call it "fine." The framework's R-06 explicitly returns Yellow (not Green) for the underpowered + non-stat-sig + CI-passes case, but Yellow is easy to dismiss. Mitigation: (1) Roll-up Yellow blocks one-click ship in the UI (forces explicit acknowledgment); (2) recommend concrete extend-the-test next action with the MDE math shown; (3) instrument adoption of the "extend" action vs Yellow ships.

Risk 3: Divergence From the Broader ExP Decision Framework

Likelihood: Medium · Impact: Medium. The source doc (Agrawal 4/27) is the broader rule-based decision layer covering SRM, primaries, segments, and LLM prompting. If this spec drifts from its decision rules, ExP ends up with two non-aligned sources of truth. Mitigation: (1) Every rule cited in Section 4 carries an explicit reference to the source-doc section it implements; (2) ExP DS owns a shared rule dictionary (R-16) used by both this framework and the parent decision layer; (3) cross-review with Ankita Agrawal before R-XX changes ship.
